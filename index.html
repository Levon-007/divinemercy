History

History of the Missionaries of St. Francis de Sales (MSFS)

The Missionaries of St. Francis de Sales (MSFS) is a Catholic religious congregation founded in France in the 19th century. Its members are priests and brothers who work especially in parish ministry, education, missions, formation, and evangelization.

Founder — Fr. Peter Marie Mermier

The congregation was founded by Fr. Peter Marie Mermier (1790–1862), a French Catholic priest. Mermier was born in Chaumont, Savoy, France, in 1790. He became a priest and was deeply influenced by the spirituality and pastoral approach of St. Francis de Sales. His desire was to renew Christian life through missions and evangelization, particularly by helping people rediscover their faith.

Influence of St. Francis de Sales

The congregation takes its name and spirituality from St. Francis de Sales (1567–1622). Francis de Sales was known for his gentle approach to evangelization and his emphasis on:

• Love of God

• Holiness in everyday life

• Patience and gentleness

• Education and formation

• Reaching people through compassionate pastoral work

Mermier wanted his missionaries to follow this spirit.

Foundation of the congregation

In 1838, Fr. Peter Marie Mermier founded the Missionaries of St. Francis de Sales at Annecy, France. The congregation's principal purpose was missionary work and the renewal of Christian communities. A central idea associated with Mermier's missionary vision was:

"To renew the spirit of Christianity in the hearts of Christians."
